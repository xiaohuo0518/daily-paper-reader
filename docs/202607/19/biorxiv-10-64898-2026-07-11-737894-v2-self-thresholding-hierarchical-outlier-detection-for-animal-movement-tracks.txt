1. Erroneous locations are ubiquitous in animal tracking data and prove notori-11

caused by poor satellite geometry, atmospheric interference, tag malfunction, or 13

# 1 Introduction 58 

Animals’ paths encode information about their physiology, behaviour, and the ways they 59 

interact with the landscapes they inhabit. Tracking technologies record these paths at 60 

unprecedented resolution, generating millions of locations annually across hundreds of 61 

species and thousands of individuals, feeding into analyses of space use, resource selection, 62 

energetics, and social behaviour (Kays et al. , 2022; Joo et al. , 2020). Like any measuring 63 

instrument used to collect data, tracking technology and the accuracy of location estima-64 

tion is associated with errors (Péron et al. , 2020). GPS fixes can be displaced by tens 65 

to hundreds of metres through poor satellite geometry, signal multipath, or obstruction 66 

(Frair et al. , 2010; Lewis et al. , 2007; D’Eon & Delparte, 2005). Argos Doppler positions, 67 

owing to the methodology of location estimation, carry errors from sub-kilometre to be-68 

yond 10 km depending on location class (Costa et al. , 2010; Vincent et al. , 2002; Lopez 69 

et al. , 2014). A more recent and increasingly pervasive source of error is deliberate GNSS 70 

jamming and more elaborately spoofing in conflict zones, where military countermeasures 71 

feed false signals to civilian GPS receivers (Jiguet et al. , 2025; Martín-Vélez et al. , 2026). 72 

Regardless of the source of noise, erroneous locations can severely distort any downstream 73 

use of location data in ecological studies. Estimates of speed, turning angle distributions, 74 

home range size, habitat selection coefficients, and behavioural state assignments can 75 

become severely affected by measurement error (Hurford, 2009; Bjørneraas et al. , 2010; 76 

Gupte et al. , 2022; Frair et al. , 2010). 77 

Screening and removing outliers is therefore one of the first and most consequential 78 

steps in any movement-analysis pipeline, yet the methods in use are largely bespoke to 79 

a given species, device, and sampling schedule, adding manual work and lowering re-80 

producibility. The simplest and most widely used are threshold filters — a maximum 81 

plausible speed (McConnell et al. , 1992), a speed–distance–angle criterion (Freitas et al. ,82 

2008; Austin et al. , 2003), or turning-angle rules (Douglas et al. , 2012; Shimada et al. ,83

anomalies but leave the removal decision to the analyst, and their estimates depend on 94 

the assumed measurement-error (UERE) calibration. The difficulty compounds on heav-95

behaviour: separating signal from noise is hard for any method, and is amplified because 97 

the errors corrupt the very metrics used to detect them. 98 

Existing deterministic approaches share a deeper limitation that is less often tackled. 99 

Filters and smoothers, whether parametric or empirical, ultimately reduce each fix to a 100 

single scalar judgment (often a binary yes-or-no outcome) about its transition to neigh-101 

bours: is the speed plausible, is the turning angle plausible, is the residual to the smoothed 102 

path plausible? Yet some consequential errors in real tracking data are spatially coherent 103 

blocks : a GPS receiver locks onto a reflected signal and produces a drift cluster of positions 104 

that sit together in the wrong place, or a spoofing/jamming event pins the receiver at a 105 

single falsified coordinate for minutes to hours (Jiguet et al. , 2025; Martín-Vélez et al. ,106 

2026). Block errors have normal interiors and extreme boundaries. A transition-based 107 

test can detect the entry and exit of the block; it is structurally blind to the interior. 108 

Visually it is no problem to see that the cluster is geometrically detached from where 109 

the animal should be, regardless of whether each within-block transition is plausible; a 110 

capability that currently lacks a method that captures this geometric anomaly. 111

Here, I introduce the primitives as the first major components of move2utils , an 120 

R package for working with move2 objects (Kranstauber et al. , 2024); the package as 121 

a whole is described in a companion Application Note (Kranstauber et al. , 2026). The 122 

first primitive, mt_flag_outliers() , is the empirical-probability method: it constructs 123 

distributions of step lengths, turning angles, and their consecutive changes from the data 124 

itself — the empirical step-and-turn distribution architecture introduced for trajectory 125 

simulation by Technitis (2021). The function adds a gap-aware auto-difference normal-126 

isation that absorbs irregular sampling,. at least to a certain degree, without assuming 127 

a movement model, and applies a self-determining threshold (MacArthur, 1957; Silver-128 

man, 1981). A complementary sequential scan ( mt_sequential_outliers() ) resolves 129 

clusters of consecutive outliers that simultaneous scoring may miss. The second primi-130 

tive, mt_flag_outliers_bridge() (Kranstauber et al. , 2012), is the geometric method: 131 

it scores each fix by its residual from the time-weighted Brownian-bridge mean of its 132 

neighbours, normalised by the bridge width, and thresholds via the same density-valley 133 

detector. The bridge score is immune to the leverage problem that afflicts variance-based 134 

geometric tests. The outlier cannot inflate its own denominator, because the denominator 135 

depends only on the temporal geometry of the bridge, not on an estimated variance at 136 

the focal point. In a directional variant (Kranstauber et al. , 2014), the residual is further 137 

decomposed into components parallel and perpendicular to the local travel axis, making 138 

the primitive an error-morphology classifier as well as a detector: perpendicular-drift er-139 

rors and along-track jumps occupy separable regions of the decomposition space and can 140 

be distinguished at a glance. 141

# 2 A cleaning cascade for movement tracks 142 

2.1 What mt_clean_track() does 143 

The function mt_clean_track() takes a movement track (a sequence of timestamped 144 

GPS positions packaged as a move2 object (Kranstauber et al. , 2024)) and returns the 145 

same track with diagnostic columns attached: a logical is_outlier flag, a categorical 146 

error_class naming the kind of evidence that triggered each flag, and the per-detector 147 

firings behind it (Figure 1d). In its default form it takes no parameters beyond the data; 148 

a physiological speed bound or a behavioural-state vector, where available, sharpen the 149 

cleaning but are not required. The output is a drop-in replacement for the input, ready 150 

for home-range estimation, step-selection models, or behavioural segmentation with the 151 

diagnostic columns retained for auditing or dropped. Figure 1 traces the whole flow: input 152 

and options at the top, the four detectors feeding the consensus rule (panel b), the two 153 

structural rescue stages that per-fix scoring cannot reach (panels a, c), and the output 154 

fingerprint that names each flag’s error class (panel d). 155 

2.2 The four ideas behind the method 156 

The cascade combines four ideas, three of which are individually not novel but whose 157 

composition is, and one, the topological block expansion, which has no equivalent in the 158 

existing animal-tracking literature. The mathematical formulations of every detector and 159 

combination rule are in Supplement S1; the four paragraphs that follow describe each idea 160 

and why it earns its place in the cascade. 161 

Data-driven thresholds. The threshold separating outliers should come from the data, 162 

not the user. Existing tools require a maximum plausible speed, a distance limit, a 163 

turn-angle cut-off, or a quality score: choices that depend on species biology, sampling 164 

regime, tag hardware, and study question, and that the user often makes by educated 165 

guess. Each detector instead scores every fix and asks whether the distribution of those 166 

scores contains a real gap between the bulk of normal movement and a tail of suspect 167

Complementary detectors. No single signal catches every kind of error. Real GPS 174 

tracks carry at least four broad classes of contamination, each demanding a different 175 

test: kinematic anomalies (an unusual speed or turn-angle pattern), geometric anomalies 176 

(a position implausibly far from where its temporal neighbours place it), biological-bound 177 

violations (an implied step speed the animal cannot physiologically produce), and symmet-178 

ric out-and-back excursions (ordinary kinematics but pathological path geometry). The 179 

cascade runs four detectors aligned to these classes — a probability detector, a Brownian-180 

bridge detector, a step-level speed cap , and a path-vs-displacement detour ratio , the four 181 

boxes feeding the consensus rule in Figure 1b — each strongest in one regime and struc-182 

turally blind in another, so that one detector’s strength covers another’s blind spot. Their 183 

scope of validity and standalone use are detailed in Section 4; their formulations are in 184 

Supplement S1. 185 

Topological block expansion. The third pillar reaches an error mode per-fix scoring 186 

structurally cannot. When a receiver locks onto a reflected signal, or a spoofing event 187 

pins it at a falsified coordinate for hours, the track carries a coherent block of erroneous 188 

positions whose interior looks locally normal (each step between neighbouring false fixes is 189 

kinematically plausible) so per-fix detectors flag only the block’s entry and exit, never its 190 

interior. After each cleaning pass the cascade therefore re-reads the kept fixes as a graph 191 

whose edges join consecutive fixes that a physiologically possible step could connect (Fig-192 

ure 1c); a coherent block falls out as a small component disconnected from the legitimate 193 

trajectory by the impossible transitions at its boundary, and every fix in a non-largest 194 

component is flagged block (the gating logic is in Supplement S1, Eqs. 18–19). The step 195

Agreement-based combination. Finally, the four verdicts must become one per-fix 201 

decision. A majority vote would treat the detectors as interchangeable witnesses, 202 

which they are not: a geometric and a kinematic witness test different properties 203 

of the same fix, and one falling silent does not weaken the other. The default rule, 204 

evidence_corroborated , instead accumulates each detector’s calibrated, signed evidence 205 

and flags a fix when the total is positive and either two detectors corroborate it or one is 206 

overwhelming on its own scale (the evidence calculus is in Supplement S1). This rewards 207 

agreement between complementary witnesses, still lets a single extreme witness act, and 208 

keeps false positives low by refusing isolated marginal votes. The same structure labels 209 

every flagged fix with what kind of error it represents — the error_class fingerprint 210 

of Figure 1d (formal predicates in Supplement S1, Table 4) — which an alternative 211 

class_aware rule can also use as the decision itself. 212

2.4 Choosing a decision rule, and what the choice costs 249 

The cohort recovers the morphologies the cascade was built for, but leaves one archi-250 

tectural choice open: the four detectors produce evidence, and the flag-to-verdict step 251 

can turn that evidence into a decision in more than one principled way. The default, 252 

evidence_corroborated , accumulates each detector’s calibrated evidence and flags on 253 

a corroborated positive total (Section S1.13); the alternative, class_aware , applies the 254 

Boolean conjunction of agreement classes (Table 4) directly. The two are equivalent in de-255 

tection skill. They both draw on the same detectors and differ only in how they pool them, 256 

and on the synthetic cohort the gap between them is far smaller than the spread induced 257 

by the random realisation alone (Supplement S5). I default to evidence_corroborated 258 

not because it detects more but because, at equal skill, it is the better-behaved object: un-259 

supervised and self-calibrating, it returns a continuous combined_evidence score rather 260 

than a bare flag, so a user can rank fixes by suspicion or set their own operating point, 261 

and it extends to new detectors without rewriting a logical table. 262

# 6 Discussion 341 

mt_clean_track() cleans animal-movement tracks with no user-supplied parameters in 342 

its default form, recovering every injected outlier on every track of the synthetic cohort, 343 

posting the highest cross-track mean F1 of deterministic tools aimed for quick outlier 344 

removal tested, and uniquely handling block-shaped contamination. Two points deserve 345 

more than the results allowed: how the directional decomposition of the bridge residual 346 

turns detection into classification, and where the limits of synthetic evaluation lie. 347 

Decomposition as error-morphology classification 348 

The directional variant of the bridge primitive, built on the dynamic bivariate Gaussian 349 

bridge (Kranstauber et al. , 2014) (Supplement S1), turns outlier detection into outlier 350 

classification . The pair (η∥, η ⊥) defines a two-dimensional diagnostic space in which error 351 

morphologies occupy separable regions: multipath and lateral spoofing fall on the per-352 

pendicular axis, timestamp glitches and forward-axis teleportation on the parallel axis, 353 

extreme events on both. A flagged fix’s position is indicative of the kind of error that 354 

produced it, changing the claims a practitioner can make: rather than “these 240 fixes 355 

are outliers,” one can report that ∼40 are perpendicular-drift events and ∼200 along-356 

axis forward jumps. The framework also works in reverse, persistent perpendicular-drift 357 

flags concentrated at one location are consistent with location-dependent multipath (the 358 

colony-halo case in CPF_E) and could be used to down-weight that location even when 359 

no single fix is an outlier on a scalar test. On the synthetic cohort this morphology map-360

the stretches of track where outliers actually live. 365 

Scalability on large tracking datasets 366 

Each detector scans the track in O(n), so the cascade scales linearly to the 10 5–10 6-fix 367 

tracks that GPS-GSM and Iridium tags now routinely produce (Supplement S4), and stays 368 

tractable on datasets where neither a Kalman-filter smoother nor a full continuous-time 369 

state-space fit (Jonsen et al. , 2023; Johnson et al. , 2008) would be. 370 

Limitations 371 

Five limitations are worth naming explicitly. The first, discussed in Section 5.4, is that 372 

the entire evaluation rests on synthetic data from one stochastic family with controlled 373 

error injection; real tracks add behavioural-regime shifts, multi-source error co-presence, 374 

and sampling irregularity that the cohort does not exercise, and only application to real 375 

deployments will establish how the cascade behaves under them. 376 

The second is that the directional variant of the bridge residual assumes the local travel 377 

axis is well defined by the two temporal neighbours of the focal fix. When a sustained error 378 

block contains the neighbours of an interior fix — they too are spoofed — the inferred 379 

travel axis tilts toward the contaminated trajectory, and the perpendicular residual of an 380 

interior fix underestimates its true sideways displacement. The combined detection mode 381 

(the default, which flags on either the scalar or the directional score) sidesteps this on 382 

synthetic ground truth, but a user relying on the directional decomposition alone for an 383 

error-morphology classification should be aware of the edge case. 384 

The third concerns the cascade’s gap-aware autodifference normalisation, which esti-385 

mates a gap-dependent scale from binned MAD values of consecutive movement-metric 386 

changes. Tracks with highly irregular sampling, in which certain gap lengths are rare, can 387

per-segment labels through the state argument (Section 3) runs the kinematic detectors 404

responsibility. 406 

Outlook 407 

The package is designed to grow. The probability framework and move2 integration are a 408 

foundation for further time-aware, non-parametric utilities — gap detection, resampling, 409 

behavioural segmentation — under one interface. The cascade is a screening step, not an 410 

error model: it returns per-fix flags and a continuous combined_evidence score rather 411 

than a smoothed trajectory, so analyses that prefer to retain and down-weight noisy fixes 412 

rather than delete them can pass these outputs to a continuous-time smoother (Johnson 413 

et al. , 2008; Calabrese et al. , 2016; Jonsen et al. , 2023) as an observation-quality input. 414 

The cascade is thus complementary to, not a replacement for, error-process models. The 415

# S1 Methods in detail 613 

S1.1 Probability components 614 

The core idea is to ask, for each location in a track, how probable the observed movement 615 

is under the empirical distribution of the track as a whole. An animal moving normally 616 

produces a characteristic signature in the joint space of speed, angular velocity, and the 617 

persistence of these quantities between consecutive steps. An erroneous location disrupts 618 

this signature: the speed to and from it is anomalously high, the angular velocity implies 619 

an abrupt directional reversal, and the changes in speed and turning rate between con-620 

secutive steps are discontinuous. By quantifying how probable each location’s movement 621 

signature is, the method identifies locations that are inconsistent with the animal’s own 622 

behaviour without requiring any prior specification of what “normal” looks like. 623

S1.3.1 Gap-based thresholding 744 

If the sorted log Pjoint values arose from a single continuous distribution, the gap sizes 745 

between consecutive sorted values would follow a predictable pattern determined by the 746 

harmonic series, as in MacArthur’s broken-stick model for species abundance (MacArthur, 747 

1957). I compute the ratio of each observed gap to its expected size under this null model. 748 

Gaps in the lower tail that substantially exceed their expectation signal a discontinuity 749 

between outliers and the bulk. 750 

This initial signal is refined through tail-decay inflection analysis. Starting from the 751 

most extreme sorted value, I sequentially remove candidate outliers and track the resulting 752 

change in tail length — the distance from the current leftmost value to the edge of the 753 

bulk distribution. Removing genuine outliers produces steep drops in tail length; upon 754 

entering the bulk, these drops become small and approximately linear. The inflection 755 

point, where the second derivative of the tail-length curve drops to the noise floor, marks 756 

the natural boundary. 757

log Pjoint distribution, requires no user-specified threshold in the statistical sense (only 759

naturally under iteration because removing outliers eliminates the break that triggered 761

S1.7 Sequential detection 931 

The simultaneous method scores every location independently against the probability 932 

surfaces and then applies a threshold. This works well for isolated outliers, where the 933 

erroneous location is bracketed by good data on both sides. Clusters of consecutive 934 

outliers — such as those produced by GPS spoofing episodes, where the receiver locks 935 

onto a false signal for minutes to hours — present a different challenge. Interior locations 936 

in the cluster are flanked by other displaced positions, making the steps between them 937 

appear unremarkable. Iterative refinement partially addresses this by peeling outliers 938 

from the cluster edges, but it requires multiple passes and can introduce false positives 939 

through cascading threshold shifts. 940 

An alternative approach avoids this problem by construction. Instead of scoring all 941 

locations simultaneously, I walk through the track from confirmed-good anchor locations, 942 

evaluating each candidate step against the same joint probability (Equation 4). Begin-943 

ning at the first location (the initial anchor), the method evaluates the step to the next 944 

candidate. If Pjoint for that step exceeds a threshold Pmin , the candidate is accepted as the 945 

new anchor and the scan advances. If not, the candidate is skipped and the next location 946 

is tried — the scan now evaluates the step from the current anchor to the location after 947

S1.9 Reference distributions 983 

By default, the probability surfaces are built from each individual’s own data, and each 984 

individual in a multi-track dataset is processed separately. This self-referencing is appro-985

S1.15 State-conditional cleaning and the global rescue pass 1110 

Real GPS tracks rarely sample a single behavioural distribution. A migrating bird’s 1111

another. 1116
